# Middle-earth Quest — Rules

Reference copy of the official rulebook (Fantasy Flight Games, 2009), restructured from the text extraction at
<https://lehi-innovation.github.io/boardgame-rules/extracted/middle-earth-quest-rules.txt>. Flavor text, the
introduction story, credits, advertisements and the index are left out; the rules text is kept as close to the
original as possible. Page numbers ("see page 14") refer to the printed rulebook. The original PDF is
`work/data_and_rules/MEQ_Rules_Web.pdf`.

## 1. Game overview

One player takes the role of **Sauron**; the remaining players take the roles of **heroes** of the West.

- Sauron uses plots, his growing influence and evil creatures to find the One Ring.
- The heroes help Gandalf learn the truth about the One Ring: they seek favor and knowledge from famous characters
  and embark on quests to foil Sauron's schemes.

### Object of the game

Each side tries to advance its story marker to the **Finale** space of the Story Track and complete the requirement
of its **Mission** card.

- Sauron's three story markers advance according to the Plot cards he has in play. He aims to advance **one** marker
  to the Finale space, or **all three** markers to **The Shadow Falls** space.
- The heroes win or lose as a team. Their story marker moves automatically every Sauron turn, slower or faster based
  on some Event and Shadow cards.
- If a side triggers the Finale but fails its Mission, the winner is decided by a final fight between a hero and the
  Ringwraiths.

### Number of players

Up to four players: one Sauron, the rest heroes. The rules assume three or four players; a two-player game uses the
changes in [§ 10.1](#101-two-player-game).

### Component overview

- **Event cards**: ongoing or immediate effects; place favor and character tokens on specific locations.
- **Shadow cards**: powerful abilities for Sauron (ambushing heroes with monsters, tempting heroes with power…).
- **Starting and Advanced Quest cards**: each hero has two Starting Quests and one Advanced Quest; rewards for
  completing their requirements.
- **Plot cards**: Sauron's activities. Three are Starting Plots, one of which starts in play.
- **Encounter cards**: situations heroes face while travelling. Five decks for the regions, one for the Free Peoples'
  Havens.
- **Peril cards**: dangers for heroes who travel to perilous locations.
- **Monster Reference cards**: attributes and special abilities of the monsters.
- **Monster Combat cards**: three decks Sauron uses to fight heroes with monsters or minions.
- **Hero cards**: each hero's own deck. They track the hero's health and are discarded during movement and combat.
- **Skill cards**: awarded during the game and added to a hero's hand. They give combat abilities and increase a
  hero's health. Same card back as Hero cards (star icon at the bottom).
- **Favor tokens**: good will of the Free Peoples. Collected from locations, characters, Encounter cards and Quests;
  spent to discard Plot cards and for other benefits.
- **Level tokens**: awarded for Quests and consulting characters; track increased attributes.
- **Damage tokens**: placed on Monster Reference cards and Minion spaces.
- **Story markers**: on the Story Track; track the advancing goals of both sides.
- **Sauron Action tokens**: placed on the Sauron Action area to track his available actions.
- **Plot markers**: mark the locations affected by Sauron's active Plot cards (where heroes must go to foil them).
- **Corruption cards**: given to heroes (usually by Shadow or Peril cards). Ongoing negative effects; discarded only
  when a hero rests (paying the listed favor) or when a card or ability allows it.
- **Item cards**: ongoing special abilities.
- **Mission cards**: one deck per side. Each side secretly draws one at setup; it tells how that side can win
  immediately if dominant during the Finale.
- **Hero sheets**: abilities and attributes of the heroes.
- **Monster tokens**: possible locations of monsters, color-coded by the regions they may be placed in.
- **Influence tokens**: the spread of Sauron's power. Influence on locations and in the Shadow Pool lets Sauron play
  powerful Shadow and Plot cards and makes locations dangerous.
- **Event marker**: marks the location affected by the current Event card.
- **Character tokens**: leaders and important people, placed by Event cards; reward heroes who consult them.
- **Minion figures**: Sauron's lieutenants. Two are on the board at the start; three come into play later.
- **Hero figures**: each hero's current location.

### Game board

area · Influence area · Peril deck space · Corruption deck space · Shadow deck space · Shadow Pool · Sauron Action
area · Plot deck space · Active Plot Track · permanently perilous locations · Shadow Strongholds (also permanently
perilous) · normal locations · Haven locations · normal paths · water paths.

## 3. Setup

1. **Choose sides**: decide who plays Sauron; the others are heroes.
2. **Place the game board**: Sauron sits within reach of the Sauron Action area.
3. **Hero setup** (while Sauron does step 4):
   1. **Choose heroes**: each hero player picks a different hero sheet and figure (random if players can't agree).
   2. **Place favor and characters**: all favor and character tokens go to the Favor and Character area.
   3. **Place hero figures** on each hero's starting location (listed on the hero sheet).
   4. **Set up hero decks**: separate the Hero cards into five Hero decks and one Skill deck. The Skill deck goes
      facedown next to the board. Each hero shuffles his own Hero deck and places it facedown next to "Life Pool" on
      his hero sheet. Components of unused heroes go back to the box.
   5. **Set up Starting Quests**: each hero randomly selects one of his two Starting Quest cards, follows its "Setup"
      instructions and places it in front of himself. The other Starting Quest is unused. Each hero also receives his
      Advanced Quest, usable only once his Starting Quest is completed.
   6. **Set up Event decks**: separate Event cards by stage (numeral on the back), shuffle each of the three decks.
   7. **Set up Encounter decks**: separate by region (five decks, jewel colors on the back) plus the Haven deck (three
      white towers); shuffle all six.
   8. **Place story markers**: the four story markers go on the "start" space of the Story Track.
4. **Sauron setup** (while heroes do step 3):
   1. **Place influence**: all influence tokens on the Influence area.
   2. **Place minion figures**: "Black Serpent" and "Mouth of Sauron" on their starting locations (listed on the Minion
      area). The other three minions are set aside.
   3. **Receive Starting Plot**: randomly select one of the three Starting Plots; the other two go back to the box.
      Place it on space "1" of the Active Plot Track and the matching plot marker on its affected location, then follow
      the card's instructions.
   4. **Set up Shadow, Corruption, Peril and Advanced Plot decks**: shuffle each, place facedown on the board.
   5. **Monster Reference and Monster Combat cards**: Reference cards faceup within reach; shuffle the three Monster
      Combat decks separately.
   6. **Place monster tokens**: separate the five token types by color, randomize each, five facedown piles near
      Sauron.
   7. **Other tokens**: damage tokens, Sauron action tokens, level tokens and the event marker within reach; plot
      markers "2" and "3" next to the Plot deck.
5. **Draw Mission cards**: shuffle the Sauron and Hero Mission decks; each side secretly draws one. The heroes (as a
   group) and Sauron may look at their own Mission at any time but don't share it. Mission cards are kept facedown;
   unused ones return to the box unseen.
6. **Start the game**: proceed to the first Sauron turn.

## 4. Turn structure

The game is played over turns until the Finale is triggered (usually by a story marker reaching the end of the track).
Starting with Sauron and proceeding clockwise, each player takes a turn: a **Sauron turn** or a **hero turn**. In a
four-player game: (1) Sauron, (2) Hero 1, (3) Hero 2, (4) Hero 3, then Sauron again.

### Sauron turn (summary)

1. **Hero Rally step**: discard all influence tokens from each hero's location.
2. **Story step**: hero story marker +2; Sauron's markers advance according to his active Plot cards. *Skipped on the
   first turn.* The first time a marker enters stage II or III, Sauron places new minions.
3. **Plot step**: Sauron may play one Plot card from his hand or discard an active Plot card. *Skipped on the first
   turn.*
4. **Event step**: Sauron draws three cards from the current stage's Event deck and resolves one. If neither side is
   dominant, he draws one card and resolves it (always the case on the first turn).
5. **Action step**: Sauron takes two actions (three in a four-player game).
6. **Hero Draw step**: each hero may draw cards from his life pool up to his fortitude.

### Hero turn (summary)

1. **Rest step**: if there is no monster token or minion in the hero's location and he is not in a Shadow
   Stronghold, he may rest. In a Haven he may also heal.
2. **Ambush step**: Sauron must choose one monster or minion in the hero's location and attack with it (combat).
3. **Travel step** (repeatable as often as the hero's hand allows):
   1. **Move** to an adjacent location by discarding the required Hero card(s).
   2. **Combat or Peril**: Sauron chooses whether the hero fights a monster/minion in his location or resolves a Peril
      card (if the location is perilous).
   3. **Explore** the current location: retrieve favor, consult characters, complete quests, and more.

   To explore the location where he started his turn, the hero may skip Move and Combat or Peril of his first Travel
   step.
4. **Encounter step**: if not in a perilous location, the hero draws three cards from his location's Encounter deck
   and resolves the lowest numbered card matching his location or region (if any).

## 5. Sauron rules

### 5.1 Important terms

- **Dominant**: a side is dominant if it has a story marker closer to the Finale space than its opponent (see
  [§ 5.5](#55-determining-dominance)).
- **Character**: tokens of influential people; give heroes favor or special benefits.
- **Current stage**: the rightmost stage of the Story Track containing at least one story marker.
- **Enemy**: a component or player of the other side. **Friendly**: of the same side.
- **Exhausted**: an exhausted combatant may not play more Combat cards for the rest of a combat.
- **Extension**: a string of adjacent locations all containing influence and connected to at least one Shadow
  Stronghold.
- **Hero**: the hero figure (and his components) and the player controlling it.
- **Influence strength**: the number of influence tokens on a Shadow Stronghold.
- **Location**: the circular spaces of the board. The colored frame gives the location's region.
- **Path**: dotted lines (or solid blue lines for water) connecting adjacent locations.
- **Shadow Stronghold**: a location with the black tower icon. Sauron places influence in extension of them.
- **Haven**: a location with the three white towers icon. A hero resting in a Haven may also heal.
- **Region**: ten regions, by color: Eriador, Rhudaur, Grey Mountains, Misty Mountains, Mirkwood, Enedwaith, Rohan,
  Gondor, Brown Lands, Mordor. The name is shown next to the matching Encounter deck space.
- **Side**: the heroes as a team, or Sauron.
- **Within [X] spaces**: adjacent locations are one space away. "Within 1 space of a hero" includes the hero's
  location and each adjacent location.
- **Region reference jewels**: cards that mention locations show colored jewels (left to right in the order of the
  locations mentioned) giving the region color of each location. Monster tokens show the jewels of the regions they
  may be placed in.

### 5.2 Hero Rally step

Each hero discards all influence tokens from his current location (unless he is in a Shadow Stronghold). Discarded
influence returns to the Influence area. Rallying disrupts extensions of Shadow Strongholds and prevents Sauron from
placing monster tokens there.

### 5.3 Story step

On every Sauron turn except the first, the hero story marker advances **two spaces**. Then each of Sauron's three
markers advances individually as indicated by his active Plot cards. If several active Plot cards advance the same
marker, resolve them one at a time (the marker advances two or three times).

If any marker reaches the **Finale** space, or if all three Sauron markers reach (or pass) **The Shadow Falls**
space, the Finale begins ([§ 9](#9-winning-the-game)).

**The Story Track** runs along the top of the board. The heroes' marker (green) represents how well the Free Peoples
hold Sauron's forces at bay. Sauron's markers: **ring** (yellow, finding the One Ring), **conquest** (red, mustering
military forces), **corruption** (black, corrupting the leaders of the Free Peoples).

**Stages**: the track is split into stages I, II and III. The current stage is the stage of the marker closest to
the Finale. It determines which Event deck Sauron draws from and the Shadow Pool capacity.

**Placing minions**: after advancing markers, the first time a marker enters stage II or III, Sauron places the
minions shown on the Story Track on their starting locations (shown on each minion's space of the board):

| Stage | Minions |
|---|---|
| I (start of game) | Mouth of Sauron, Black Serpent |
| II | Ringwraiths, Gothmog of Gorgoroth |
| III | Witch-king |

*Example*: the hero marker advances two spaces. Sauron has two active Plot cards, each advancing his corruption
marker one space. This is the first time a marker enters stage III, so the current stage becomes III and Sauron
immediately places the Witch-king on one of its starting locations.

### 5.4 Plot step

Skipped on the first turn. Sauron may do one of:

- **Play a Plot card**: fulfil its requirements, place it on an empty space of the Active Plot Track, and place the
  matching plot marker on the affected location. If the Active Plot Track is full, he may not play a Plot card.
- **Discard an active Plot card** (typically to make room for another one on a later turn).
- **Do nothing**.

#### Plot cards

During each Story step, Sauron's markers advance the number of spaces shown on each active Plot card.

- **Influence requirement** (upper left): the minimum influence in the Shadow Pool to play the card. Playing it does
  not spend that influence.
- **Requirements**: italic text at the top of the text box (e.g. "Requires a monster token or minion adjacent to the
  Woodland Realm"). Once in play, requirements are ignored: the card stays even if they are no longer met.
- **Cost to discard**: favor a hero must spend while exploring the affected location to discard the card.
- **Story marker advancement**: while on the Active Plot Track, the matching marker advances this many spaces each
  Story step.
- **Game text**: requirements, affected location, special rules while active.

**Affected locations**: when a Plot card is played, its plot marker goes on the affected location (where heroes must
go to foil it).

**Discarding active Plot cards**: a hero explores the affected location and spends the favor shown on the card.
Discarded Plot cards go faceup to a discard pile next to the Plot deck; the plot marker is removed. Sauron may also
discard one voluntarily during his Plot step.

### 5.5 Determining dominance

1. The heroes count the spaces from their marker to the Finale space.
2. Sauron uses the lower of: the spaces from his closest marker to the Finale space, and the cumulative spaces needed
   for all his markers to reach The Shadow Falls space.
3. The lower number is dominant. A tie means no side is dominant.

*Example*: heroes are 4 spaces from the Finale. Sauron's closest marker is 5 spaces away, but his other two markers
need only one space each to reach The Shadow Falls: Sauron's count is 2, so Sauron is dominant.

### 5.6 Event step

1. **Draw and resolve**:
   - heroes dominant: draw three Event cards from the current stage's deck, resolve the **lowest** priority number;
   - Sauron dominant: draw three, resolve the **highest** priority number;
   - neither dominant: draw the top card and resolve it.

   Other drawn cards go faceup to that deck's discard pile.
2. **Place characters and favor**: for each favor listed at the bottom of the card, take a favor token from the Favor
   and Character area and place it on the specified location. For each character listed, place that character token
   on the designated location. A character already on another location stays where it is.
3. **Keep in play or discard**: a card with the event marker icon (green flag, upper right) goes on the Current Event
   Card space, replacing any prior card. Otherwise it is discarded.

**Anatomy**: priority number (higher numbered cards tend to benefit the heroes more), name and flavor, event marker
icon, ability (resolved immediately, or when its requirements are met if it affects a location), favor and characters.

**Affected locations**: when a card with a location goes on the Current Event Card space, move the event marker to
that location. When the card is discarded (usually after a hero explores there), the event marker is removed. If a
hero explores the location but doesn't activate the card's ability, the card stays. Favor and characters placed by a
discarded Event card stay where they are.

**Hero decision making**: when cards ask the heroes to decide as a side, they must agree; otherwise decide randomly.
Heroes may discuss but not show their hands. Sauron hears all hero conversation.

### 5.7 Action step

Sauron takes **two actions** (**three** in a four-player game). For each action he places an action token on the
**leftmost empty space** of one of the three Action Tracks and resolves that action with X = the number on the space
just covered. Each track has three spaces with decreasing numbers.

While three or fewer tokens are on the board they stay, limiting Sauron's choices. When Sauron places his **fourth**
token, he immediately removes the three prior tokens, leaving only the one just placed.

*Example*: tokens are already on the "6" and "5" spaces of Place Influence. Sauron places a token on "4" of Place
Influence (up to two influence to the Shadow Pool, the rest in extension). His second action covers "3" of Command
Monsters and Minions. That is his fourth token, so he removes all tokens except this last one.

#### Action: Place Influence

Sauron takes X influence tokens. He may place **up to two** in the Shadow Pool and the rest in extension of his Shadow
Strongholds.

*Example*: on the "6" space, Sauron takes six influence. One goes to the Shadow Pool. One goes on the Sea of Rhûn
(traceable back to Barad-dûr through influenced locations). Two go on Barad-dûr (now 3 tokens: each location in its
extension may hold up to 3). The last two go on the Dead Marshes (now connected to Barad-dûr, whose strength is at
least 2).

#### Action: Draw Shadow and Plot Cards

Sauron draws X Shadow cards and X Plot cards. The Shadow cards go to his hand. He keeps **one** of the drawn Plot
cards and puts the others facedown at the bottom of the Plot deck in the order of his choice.

#### Action: Command Monsters and Minions

Sauron gets X commands. For each command, one of:

- **Move monster or minion** to an adjacent location (ignoring movement icons). Monster tokens may only move onto
  locations with at least one influence; minions may move anywhere, including Havens (but Sauron can't start a combat
  against a hero in a Haven unless the hero allows it).
- **Place monster token**: choose one of the five piles, draw a random token, look at it secretly, place it facedown
  on a location matching the token's color. Restrictions: only on a location with at least one influence; only
  **one** monster token placed per Command action (whatever the number of commands); never on a location containing
  a hero (they may be moved onto a hero's location). Several monster tokens and minions may share a location.
- **Heal minion**: remove up to four damage tokens from a minion. Not allowed if the minion is on the same location
  as a hero.

Each monster token and minion receives **at most one command per action**: a token can't be moved by the action that
placed it, and nothing moves twice with the same action.

All combat happens during hero turns: a monster or minion moved into a hero's location only fights at that hero's
next Ambush step.

### 5.8 Influence

**Shadow Pool**: influence there lets Sauron play Shadow and Plot cards (requirement printed upper left; playing a
card doesn't spend influence). Capacity: **4 × the current stage** (4 in stage I, 8 in stage II, 12 in stage III).

**Influence on locations** lets Sauron place and move monster tokens, makes locations perilous for heroes, and helps
fulfil Plot requirements.

**Placing influence on locations** with a Place Influence action: always in extension of a Shadow Stronghold, i.e.
Sauron must trace a contiguous path of influenced locations back to a Shadow Stronghold. Influence may go on the
Stronghold itself, on a location already in the extension, or on a location adjacent to the extension. Influence may
**never** be placed in Havens.

**Maximum influence**:

- A Shadow Stronghold's influence strength is its number of influence tokens; the red number on its icon is the
  maximum it may hold.
- A location in extension can't hold more influence than the strength of its connected Stronghold (the highest one if
  connected to several).
- Excess influence is removed immediately, down to a minimum of one.
- If heroes cut a chain, every location no longer in extension keeps only one influence token.

### 5.9 Shadow cards

Usually drawn with the Draw Shadow and Plot Cards action. To play one, Sauron needs at least the required influence in
the Shadow Pool and must follow its timing (e.g. "Play at the start of combat": before any combat step). **Only one
Shadow card per player's turn** (in a four-player game: up to four per round, one in his own turn and one in each hero
turn).

**Anatomy**: Shadow Pool requirement, title, timing restriction (italic), game effect (resolved immediately).

### 5.10 Minions

Five plastic figures, Sauron's strongest lieutenants, defending his plots. Black Serpent and Mouth of Sauron start in
play; the others enter in stages II and III. Their attributes and special abilities are printed on their Minion space
of the board, where their damage tokens are kept until healed or defeated.

### 5.11 Monster tokens

Placed with the Command action on influenced locations. 60 % are real monsters, 40 % are blank (false rumors). Once on
the board, Sauron may look at a token at any time. No limit to monster tokens or minions in a location.

### 5.12 Hero Draw step

Each hero draws cards from his life pool equal to his fortitude. No hand limit (some Shadow cards force heroes to
discard down to five). Heroes may draw fewer cards.

## 6. Hero rules

### 6.1 Rest step

To rest, the hero shuffles all cards of his **rest pool** into his **life pool**, then advances the **leftmost**
(lowest) Sauron story marker one space (on a tie, the hero chooses which). No resting if a monster or minion is on the
hero's location or if he is on a Shadow Stronghold.

- **Discarding Corruption cards**: whenever a hero rests, he may discard one Corruption card by spending the favor
  shown in its lower right corner.
- **Healing**: resting in a Haven, the hero also shuffles his **damage pool** into his life pool.

### 6.2 Hero sheet

1. **Combat chart**: ratio of Ranged (blue) to Melee (red) cards in the deck.
2. **Ability**: the hero's special ability.
3. **Starting location**.
4. **Attributes**:
   - **Fortitude**: cards drawn in the Hero Draw step.
   - **Strength**: limits the cards played in combat (each card has a strength cost).
   - **Agility**: at combat start, split between drawing Hero cards and adding to strength.
   - **Wisdom**: avoiding perilous locations; affects many Encounter cards. A location is perilous for a hero if its
     influence **exceeds** his wisdom.

### 6.3 Hero card pools

- **Life pool**: facedown deck; the only pool a hero normally draws from. If it is empty and the hand is empty, the
  hero is defeated. Its size is public.
- **Rest pool**: faceup. Cards discarded (e.g. for travel) go on top. Resting shuffles it into the life pool. Anyone
  may look at it without reordering it.
- **Damage pool**: facedown. For each damage, the hero discards one card from his **hand or life pool, in any
  combination**. Healing shuffles it into the life pool. Its size is public.

### 6.4 Ambush step

If the hero's location contains monster tokens and/or minions, Sauron must choose one of them to fight the hero. If
the hero is defeated in this combat his turn ends; otherwise it continues. In a Haven, combat only happens if the hero
allows it.

### 6.5 Travel step

Repeatable as many times as the hero wishes or can (usually limited by his hand, or ended by a defeat in combat).

#### a) Move

Each path shows a movement icon and a number. Six icons: hill, mountain, forest, plains, swamp, any card. To move,
the hero discards **one card with the matching icon**, or **as many cards as the number** on the path. Discarded cards
go faceup to the rest pool.

- **Water paths** (blue): normal paths, but with a "Boat" Item card the hero crosses them by discarding any one card.
- **Any card icon**: discard any one Hero card.

*Example*: a "hill (3)" path costs one hill card or any three cards.

#### b) Combat or Peril

After moving to a location that is perilous and/or contains monster tokens or minions, Sauron must resolve one of the
following (if able):

- **Monster or minion attack**: Sauron flips a monster token of his choice or chooses a minion there; the hero fights
  it. If the hero doesn't defeat it, **his turn ends immediately** (unlike the Ambush step). A blank monster token is
  discarded and the hero proceeds to Explore.
- **Peril**: if the location is perilous, Sauron draws three Peril cards and chooses one that affects the hero's
  location/region for the hero to resolve. All three are then discarded. If none affects the location or region, all
  three are discarded without effect.

#### Peril cards

A location is **perilous** if it is permanently perilous (peril frame) or contains more influence than the hero's
wisdom. Each Peril card names its affected location(s); Sauron may only choose cards matching the hero's location
("Any Location" cards always match). The card effect is resolved by the hero.

#### Travel example

Argalad discards a forest card to move from the Woodland Realm to the Forest Trail: no monster, not perilous, so he
skips Combat or Peril. Exploring, he consults Gandalf and takes two favor; Gandalf returns to the Favor and Character
area. He then discards a mountain card to go to the High Pass, which holds more influence than his wisdom: Sauron
draws three Peril cards and chooses one. Argalad doesn't explore and travels again: with no mountain card left he
discards three cards to reach the Gladden Fields. A monster token is there: Sauron flips it (an Orc) and Argalad
fights. If he fails to defeat the Orc his turn ends; otherwise he continues (exploring the Gladden Fields).

#### c) Explore

The hero may do any or all of the following, in any order:

- **Retrieve favor**: any number of favor tokens on the location.
- **Consult with characters**: for each character on the location, gain its favor **or** use its ability (not both).
  The character returns to the Favor and Character area.
- **Choose a Dark Path**: if the hero has 3 or fewer Corruption cards, he may gain 1 favor and 1 Corruption card.
  Once per turn.
- **Complete a Quest**: any number whose requirements are met.
- **Discard a Plot**: discard Sauron's active Plot cards affecting this location by paying their discard cost
  (usually favor).
- **Trade with heroes**: all heroes on the location may freely trade favor, Item cards and Quests (not Starting or
  Advanced Quests), even if they already played this round.

#### Favor tokens

Gained from quests, characters and locations (from the location, or from the Favor and Character area when provided
by a card) and placed on the hero sheet.

#### Character tokens

Start in the Favor and Character area; Event and Quest cards place them on locations. When consulting, the hero
either **uses the ability** printed on the token (often a level or another reward) or **gains the favor** listed on
it. The token then returns to the Favor and Character area.

### 6.6 Encounter step

After travelling, if not in a perilous location, the hero **must** draw three cards from the Encounter deck matching
his location's color (Haven deck if in a Haven). He resolves the card with the **lowest priority number** that affects
his location or region; the others are discarded faceup. If none matches, all are discarded and his turn ends. Most
Encounter cards resolve immediately; a card marked *Quest* goes faceup in the hero's play area. An empty Encounter deck
is rebuilt by shuffling its discard pile.

**Anatomy**: priority number, affected location, region colors, title and flavor, effect.

#### Quests

Quests come from the Starting/Advanced Quest decks and Encounter decks (*Quest* in italics). A hero may hold any
number of uncompleted Quests. When the requirements (bullet points) are met he receives the reward; completed Quests
are removed from the game. Unless stated otherwise, Quests are completed during Explore. A Quest belongs to the hero
displaying it; non-Starting/Advanced Quests may be traded.

## 7. Other game elements

### Level tokens

Mark a hero's increased attribute. "Gain one wisdom" gives a wisdom token one higher than the current value, placed
over the attribute. Each attribute may be increased **at most twice** per game (eight increases in total); a third
increase is simply lost. Tokens are double-sided.

### Skill cards

Gained through **training**: draw the top two Skill cards, put one in hand, discard the other faceup next to the Skill
deck. A Skill card is then a Hero card in every respect (playable, increases maximum health).

### Item cards

Permanent abilities. When gained, the hero takes the named card from the Item deck and places it faceup. A hero may
hold only one Item of each title.

### Corruption cards

Given by Shadow, Event and Encounter cards, or chosen through a Dark Path. Ongoing negative effects; many Shadow cards
are stronger against corrupted heroes. Discarded when resting by spending the favor shown (lower right), or for free by
some Event and Encounter cards (especially in Havens).

## 8. Combat

A combat is always one hero against one monster or minion, over **rounds**: each side chooses a card facedown, both
reveal simultaneously, effects resolve and damage is dealt. Combat ends when a combatant is defeated (0 health) or both
are exhausted. **No combat in Havens unless the hero allows it.** "Combat cards" means Monster Combat cards and Hero
cards (including Skill cards).

### 8.1 Monster Reference card and Minion spaces

- **Health**: damage needed to defeat the monster.
- **Name**, **combat chart** (ranged/melee ratio).
- **Combat deck**: Zealot, Ravager ("Marauder" in one place of the rulebook) or Behemoth.
- **Fortitude**: Monster Combat cards Sauron draws at combat start.
- **Strength**: if the total strength cost of the cards played exceeds it, the combatant becomes exhausted.
- **Wisdom**: influence Sauron may place in extension of Shadow Strongholds at the end of combat if this combatant was
  not defeated.
- **Special ability**: triggered from Ranged (blue) or Melee (red) cards.

Minions have no reference card: their data is printed on their Minion space; their attributes work the same way.

### 8.2 Combat card anatomy

Type (Melee: red, crossed swords; Ranged: blue, bow) · attack value (top center) · defense value (shields, top right) ·
ability (name and text) · strength cost (lower left) · owner (deck name, skill icon or hero portrait) · movement icon
(Hero and Skill cards only, lower right).

### 8.3 Steps of combat

1. **Sauron setup**: Sauron takes the Monster Reference card (for a monster) and the matching Monster Combat deck.
2. **Preparation**: Sauron draws cards equal to his combatant's fortitude. The hero announces how much of his agility
   he spends to draw cards from his life pool and draws them; **each unspent agility gives +1 strength** until the end
   of the combat.
3. **Rounds of combat** (repeat until a combatant is defeated or both are exhausted):
   1. **Choose cards**: each combatant puts one card from his hand facedown. An exhausted combatant skips this.
   2. **Reveal cards**: flip simultaneously and place on top of the combat stack. An exhausted combatant reveals
      nothing: zero attack, zero defense, no ability, neither melee nor ranged.
   3. **Calculate strength**: each side totals the strength cost of its combat stack. If it **exceeds** the
      combatant's strength, he becomes exhausted and his card is canceled. If both become exhausted here, neither is
      defeated and play goes to Resolution.
   4. **Previous round abilities**: "Next Round" abilities of last round's cards resolve now, hero first, then Sauron.
      A combatant who became exhausted this round loses his previous-round ability.
   5. **Current round abilities**: abilities of this round's cards resolve, hero first, then Sauron.
   6. **Deal damage**: each combatant is simultaneously dealt the opponent's total attack minus his own defense. If a
      combatant is defeated, go to Resolution; otherwise start a new round.
4. **Resolution**:
   1. **Discard cards**: the hero puts his combat stack (in order) faceup on his rest pool. Sauron shuffles his stack
      back into the Monster Combat deck.
   2. **Discard monster token**: a monster token involved in the combat is discarded faceup next to the unused tokens,
      **whether or not it was defeated**. Undefeated minions stay on their location.
   3. **Undefeated monster or minion**: Sauron may place influence equal to its **wisdom** in extension of Shadow
      Strongholds. If this happens during the hero's Travel step, the hero's turn ends.

### 8.4 Combat keywords

- **Cancel**: all text on the card is ignored, its final attack and defense are zero and can't be raised. Its strength
  cost is still paid. Two cards canceling each other are both blank. A canceled card's "next round" ability is lost.
- **Deal [X] damage**: direct damage ignoring defense, dealt when the ability resolves; combatants can only be defeated
  during Deal Damage.
- **Modifying attack or defense** ("+1 attack"): modifies the final values for Deal Damage.
- **If opponent's printed (attack or defense) is [X]**: always uses the printed value, never the modified one.
  *Example*: "Gain +5 attack if your opponent's printed defense is 0" against a card with 0 defense and "Gain +2
  defense": the hero gains +5 attack and the monster gains +2 defense.
- **Reduce to 0**: the value can't be increased above zero afterwards.
- **Combat stack**: all cards a combatant played this combat, tracking spent strength and the previous round's card.
  Some cards add or remove cards from it.
- **Types**: Ranged (blue) or Melee (red).

### 8.5 Combat example

*Round 1*: Argalad fights an Orc (Zealot deck). Sauron draws five cards (Orc fortitude 5). Argalad spends one agility
to draw one card and adds the remaining two to his strength (total 7). Both play and reveal; each spent 2 strength, no
one is exhausted. No previous-round abilities. Sauron's card reduces Argalad's defense to 0; Argalad's ability helps
next round (the Orc's "Fanatical" ability would also resolve now if able). Argalad takes 2 damage (discards two cards
from his life pool to his damage pool); the Orc takes 1 (2 attack − 1 defense).

*Round 2*: the Orc has now spent 5 strength (> 4): exhausted, card canceled. Argalad has spent 5 (≤ 7). His
previous-round ability gives +2 attack (he played a Ranged card). His current ability (+3 attack against Melee) doesn't
trigger, as the exhausted Orc played no Melee card. The Orc has 0 defense and takes 5 damage (3 + 2). With 6 damage
tokens against 3 health it is defeated; go to Resolution.

### 8.6 Combat timing

If several cards resolve at the same time, the hero's card always resolves first.

### 8.7 Exhausted combatants

A combatant whose combat stack's total strength cost exceeds his strength becomes exhausted; the card just played is
canceled. The other combatant keeps playing rounds until one is defeated or both are exhausted. **Before any round, a
combatant with no cards in hand (or who doesn't want to play) declares himself exhausted**; if both want to, the hero
declares first, then Sauron decides. Exhaustion lasts for the rest of the combat.

### 8.8 Damage and defeat

- **Monsters**: one damage token per damage on the Monster Reference card. Defeated when damage ≥ health during Deal
  Damage; the token is removed and placed faceup next to the unused tokens.
- **Minions**: damage tokens on the Minion space **persist after combat** until healed or defeated. When defeated,
  damage is cleared and the figure removed. Defeated minions don't return, **except the Ringwraiths, who return to
  Minas Morgul at the start of Sauron's next Action step**.
- **Heroes**: for each damage, discard the top card of the life pool (unseen) or a card of his choice from hand,
  facedown to the damage pool one at a time. A hero with zero cards in life pool and hand is immediately defeated.

### 8.9 Defeated heroes

1. **Advance Sauron's story**: the leftmost Sauron marker advances one space (the hero chooses among ties).
2. **Lose favor or Item card**: the hero discards one favor or one Item card (his choice); skipped if he has neither.
3. **Move to Haven**: the figure moves to the **closest Haven by number of locations**; Sauron breaks ties.
4. **Recover**: shuffle the rest pool and damage pool into the life pool.
5. **End turn**: the turn ends immediately.

## 9. Winning the game

At the end of the Story step, the **Finale** begins if a story marker reached the Finale space, or if all three Sauron
markers entered (or passed) The Shadow Falls space.

### Mission cards

Each side draws one at setup and keeps it secret. Parts: title, picture, mission requirement (wins the game, or
advances story markers during the Finale).

| Side | Mission | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Sauron | His Dark Throne | 3 active Plot cards in play |
| Sauron | In Darkness Bind Them | yellow (ring) marker in stage III |
| Sauron | To Rule Them All | red (conquest) marker in stage III |
| Sauron | Where the Shadows Lie | black (corruption) marker in stage III |
| Sauron | To Find Them All | the Ringwraiths, or 6 influence, on the Shire |
| Heroes | Noble Blood | heroes collectively have 1 or fewer Corruption cards |
| Heroes | Against the Shadow | no more than 2 monster tokens in play |
| Heroes | Isildur's Secret | heroes collectively have at least 5 favor |
| Heroes | Minas Morgul Kept at Bay | 2 or fewer minions in play |
| Heroes | The Spear of the West | each hero completed both his Starting and Advanced Quest |

### Finale

Players stop taking normal turns and follow these steps:

1. **Check for immediate victory**: the dominant side reveals its Mission; if fulfilled, it wins. If neither side is
   dominant, both reveal; if exactly one fulfils its Mission, it wins. Otherwise continue.
2. **Advance story markers**: if Sauron fulfils his Mission, each of his markers advances one space; if the heroes
   fulfil theirs, their marker advances one space. A marker on the Finale space doesn't advance.
3. **Prepare**: remove all damage from the Ringwraiths. Each hero shuffles hand, rest pool and damage pool into his
   life pool, then draws cards equal to his fortitude.
4. **Adjust difficulty**:
   - Sauron dominant: the Ringwraiths gain health and fortitude equal to the hero marker's distance to the Finale.
   - Heroes dominant: the Ringwraiths lose health and fortitude equal to the lower of the closest Sauron marker's
     distance to the Finale and the cumulative spaces Sauron's markers need to reach The Shadow Falls.
5. **Choose champion**: the heroes choose one hero (they already drew, so they can pick the best hand).
6. **Combat**: the champion fights the Ringwraiths with all standard combat rules.
7. **Determine winner**: Ringwraiths defeated → heroes win; otherwise Sauron wins.

*Example*: the hero marker reaches the Finale. The heroes are dominant but don't fulfil their Mission. Sauron fulfils
his, so his markers advance one space. His closest marker is 3 spaces from the Finale; his markers need 5 spaces in
total to reach The Shadow Falls (2 yellow + 3 red). The lower number (3) gives the Ringwraiths −3 health and −3
fortitude.

## 10. Other rules

### 10.1 Two-player game

- The hero player gets **two turns per Sauron turn**: after his first Encounter step, resolve a special Hero Rally
  step and a Hero Draw step, then he takes a second turn starting with his Rest step. After the second Encounter step,
  Sauron's turn begins normally (with the Hero Rally step).
- Sauron may play one Shadow card during each of the hero's two turns.

### 10.2 Reshuffling

When a card deck or monster token pile is depleted, reshuffle its discards into a new deck/pile.

### 10.3 Component limitations

Favor, influence and damage tokens are unlimited (use replacements). All other components are strictly limited.

### 10.4 Optional rules (all players must agree before the game)

- **Advanced dominance**: a side is dominant if, simulating Story steps with Sauron's active Plot cards staying in
  play, it would reach the Finale in fewer Story steps. *Example*: heroes 5 spaces away need 3 Story steps; Sauron's
  plots advance black and red 2 spaces each and need 4 Story steps: heroes dominant.
- **High stakes stage III**: if the heroes are dominant in stage III, Sauron chooses which marker advances each time
  he defeats a hero (instead of the lowest). If Sauron is dominant in stage III, the heroes advance their marker one
  space each time they defeat a minion.

## 11. Frequently overlooked rules

- Sauron may play at most one Shadow card during each player's turn, including his own.
- Minions may not be healed while in a hero's location.
- Sauron may not place monster tokens in a hero's location (but may move one there from an adjacent location).
- If all three Active Plot Track spaces are full, Sauron may not play a Plot card; he may discard one instead.
- Quest cards are removed from the game after completion.
- A hero is only defeated when he has no cards left in his life pool **and** his hand.
- Heroes may not rest on Shadow Stronghold locations.
- Heroes may not draw Encounter cards on perilous locations.
- Water paths are normal paths, but easier with a "Boat" Item card.

## 12. Strategy tips

**Heroes**: favor is the most precious commodity; usually take favor over other benefits. Don't forget the Dark Path
(useful, but risky). Don't let Sauron keep Plot cards in play, even when winning: plots get easier for him later. Don't
fear defeat when a risk can stop a Plot, but resting or being defeated too often makes Sauron win quickly.

**Sauron**: you win by playing Plot cards and keeping them in play; each turn, prepare to play a Plot on the next one.
Play Plots far from the heroes. Move minions onto plot marker locations. Monsters and minions are tools to defend plots
and slow heroes, not goals. Use Shadow cards to keep heroes on their toes.

## 13. Quick reference

**Combat steps**: Sauron setup → Preparation → Rounds (Choose cards, Reveal, Calculate strength, Previous round
abilities, Current round abilities, Deal damage) → Resolution (Discard cards, Discard monster token, Undefeated
monster or minion).

**Sauron turn**: Hero Rally → Story (not on turn 1) → Plot (not on turn 1) → Event → Action (2, or 3 with four
players) → Hero Draw.

**Hero turn**: Rest (heal in a Haven) → Ambush → Travel (Move, Combat or Peril, Explore; repeatable) → Encounter (not
on perilous locations).
